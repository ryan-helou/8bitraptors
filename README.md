## What it is

I built a media company that runs itself. Each page tells stories about its team the way a fan would ("we", "us", "our"), dressed up as an old handheld video game: pixel players, dialog boxes, battle screens and trainer cards. A reel might be a trade debate ("Trade Court: who won the Pascal trade?"), a moment from our history, a "choose your starter" poll, or the whole bench drawn as a team of monsters.

## How a reel gets made

1. A script is written for one story, in fan voice, with every number tied to a source.
2. The reel is drawn frame by frame from one of 19 pixel-art formats, voiced, scored with 8-bit music and kept short.
3. A fact checker compares every number on screen and in the caption with ESPN. If anything is off, the reel is not allowed to post.
4. A second, independent review watches the finished reel and rates two things: does the opening give you a reason to stay, and does the ending pay it off?
5. It joins the queue, and a cloud poster publishes it at its time slot, even when my computer is asleep.

## How it gets better on its own

- Every morning it pulls each post's views, shares, saves and how much of the reel people actually watched, then scores every format, posting hour and length against the page's average.
- The best formats get the best slots for the next week. One slot in five is kept for testing a new format or a new time, so it keeps learning instead of repeating itself.
- Twice a week it writes and builds new reels in whatever is winning, and runs one experiment: two versions of the same reel with different openings, shown only to non-followers, so the better opening goes to the feed.
- It watches team news all day. A trade, a signing or a career night becomes its own reel the same day.
- It studies what is working for other NBA fan pages and borrows the idea, never the art.
- It checks its own health every day and tells me when something breaks.
